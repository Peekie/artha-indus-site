North Sahyadri range · Maharashtra

Warli

Warli art is perhaps the most mathematically pure vernacular in South Asia. Its visual grammar relies exclusively on three primordial shapes: the circle (sun and moon), the triangle (mountains and pointed trees), and the line.

Curatorial potential: Warli avoids spatial perspective entirely, relying instead on rhythmic, non-hierarchical choreography across open ground. When scaled up into monumental bas-reliefs in cast mineral plaster or carved white oak, its dynamic human networks transform into striking, minimalist spatial friezes that celebrate community and kinetic equilibrium.

Mithila region · Bihar

Madhubani

From the Mithila region of Bihar, Madhubani is characterized, in its kachni line tradition, by double-line boundaries filled with precise cross-hatching, stylized botanical flourishes, and stark symbolic clarity.

Curatorial potential: Unlike forms that depend on tonal shading, Madhubani relies on crisp, calligraphic line tension. When isolated and enlarged, its iconic botanical archetypes, such as the sacred lotus (padma) and the fertility grove, provide high-contrast linear anchors suited for waterjet-cut metal partitions (jalis) and etched bronze panels.

Forested landscapes · Madhya Pradesh

Gond

Rooted in the forested landscapes of Madhya Pradesh, Gond expression is defined by signature micro-line matrices: repetitive herringbone dashes, stippled dots, and fluid undulating scales that animate flora and fauna.

Curatorial potential: Gond’s structural strength lies in its natural multi-scalar hierarchy. The macro-silhouette anchors the architectural volume, while the micro-textures catch natural raking light. This visual depth mirrors natural scene statistics, making it an ideal candidate for acoustic wall panels and CNC-fluted timber screens that provide visual focus without cognitive fatigue.

Coromandel coast · Andhra Pradesh

Srikalahasti Kalamkari

Along the Coromandel coast, Kalamkari artisans drew the kairi freehand using bamboo pens (kalam), suspending it as abundant fruit within the branches of the Kalpavriksha (Tree of Life). The lineage’s elemental gesture is the fluid, continuous curve of hand-drawn flora.

Saura community · Odisha

Saura

Practiced by the Saura community of Odisha, Ikons are ritual wall murals structured within a strict rectangular frame (Anital) that serves as a spiritual portal.

Curatorial potential: Saura compositions exhibit an innate architectural discipline. The linear placement of figures along horizontal tiers and vertical ladders creates an organic rhythm that naturally aligns with the post-and-beam logic of modern interiors. Rendered in monochromatic plaster relief, it introduces a stark, graphic balance to high-volume arrival foyers.

Cloth-scroll traditions · Odisha & Bengal

Pattachitra

The cloth-scroll traditions of Odisha and Bengal offer a bridge between folk vitality and classical iconographic discipline. Characterized by crisp calligraphic linework, elaborate ornamental borders, and defined spatial registers, Pattachitra has deep roots in stone temple sculpture.

Curatorial potential: The sweeping, continuous outlines of Pattachitra carry an innate sculptural weight. By isolating its primary heroic figures and architectural framing devices from dense micro-narratives, curators can translate its dynamic gestures into large-scale stone bas-reliefs and cast bronze panels that anchor executive boardrooms and gallery spaces.
