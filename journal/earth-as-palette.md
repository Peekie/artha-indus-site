Before a lineage has a style, it has a geology. The five heaps on the grinding slab above, lapis blue, red ochre, marigold yellow, raw sienna, crushed shell white, are not a designer's color scheme. They are minerals and earths, ground by the muller resting beside them, bound with water from the brass bowl, and their availability once decided what a region's art could look like at all. Color (*varṇa*) was not chosen from a fan deck. It was dug, gathered, burned, and ground.

## Where the Palette Comes From

The pattern repeats across the traditions we steward. Warli's white is rice paste on an earthen ground; its entire chromatic world is the wall itself plus one luminous line. Sohrai works in soft clay pigments because Hazaribagh's soil offers them. And at the luxurious extreme, our craft essay on bas-relief names the celebratory register directly: pigments inspired by ground lapis lazuli, deep malachite, crushed ochre, and burnished gold, the ritual opulence of courts and temples that could afford stones carried across mountains. Victoria Finlay's history of the palette follows lapis from Afghan mines to the painters of Europe and Asia; scarcity, not preference, is what first made blue precious.

## Why Mineral Color Reads as Luxury

Stand a mineral palette beside a synthetic one and the difference is not saturation. Synthetic color is more saturated. The difference is behavior.

- **Body:** Ground minerals have particle size and mass. Laid on a surface they sit *in* it rather than on it, with the faint granularity visible in the heaps above.
- **Age:** Earths and stones shift slowly and gracefully; an ochre mellows where a bright synthetic dye simply dies. A palette that ages well is a palette a building can commit to.
- **Restraint:** A mineral palette is finite, and finitude disciplines composition. The lineages built their harmony inside a dozen earths, which is one reason their work sits so calmly in contemporary interiors.

> A fan deck offers ten thousand colors and no reasons. A grinding slab offers five, and every one of them is an argument.

## The Palette in Practice

Honesty matters here, so let it be said plainly: our artisans today work largely in artist-grade acrylics, as most living practitioners across these lineages do. The tube of indigo photographed beside the peacock scroll in our essay on romance and materiality is exactly that. What survives from the grinding slab is not the chemistry but the logic. The two registers our craft essay defines, the monochromatic serenity of charcoal, bone, raw earth and slate for restorative spaces, and the celebratory richness of lapis, malachite and gold for grand hospitality, are mineral palettes in spirit: bounded, grounded, and chosen for how they hold light rather than how loudly they leave a swatch card. When a specifier asks why the work never looks trendy, this is the honest answer. The palette was never fashionable to begin with. It was geological, and the geology still sets the terms even when the paint comes from a tube.
