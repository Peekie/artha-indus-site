Look closely at the sheet above and the order of work reveals itself. The red grid came first: verticals, horizontals, diagonals, each interval annotated in fractions along the margins. The figure came second, drawn into a cage of measure that was waiting for it. The bamboo ruler resting beside the paper is not an accessory to this tradition. It is the tradition.

The sacred Tibetan and Himalayan Buddhist scroll tradition operates on the strictest system of proportional iconometry (*tālamāna*). Its geometric grid networks, concentric mandalas, and precise directional vectors offer a rigorous framework for creating centered, contemplative focal points. Nothing about that framework is improvised. Jackson and Jackson's standard study of thangka method documents grids handed from master to student in which the face, the torso, and the seated width of a figure are fixed as ratios of a single module, so that a painting made in one valley agrees with a painting made in another.

## Measure as Inheritance

It is worth pausing on what a proportional system actually preserves. Not the image: images vary with every hand. What the grid preserves is the *relationship* between parts, and relationships survive changes of scale, material, and century in a way that outlines do not. Kapila Vatsyayan's work on the square and the circle in the Indian arts traces this intuition across dance, sculpture, and architecture: the module, not the motif, is the true unit of transmission.

This is also, quietly, a description of how a modern building gets made. Contemporary architecture runs on modular coordination, repeating dimensional units that let a facade panel, a ceiling grid, and a partition system agree with one another. The vocabularies differ; the instinct is the same one the iconometrists formalized centuries earlier.

## What the Grid Means for Translation

For a curation house, *tālamāna* is more than a scholarly curiosity. It is the reason sacred geometry translates to architecture at all.

- **Scale independence:** A composition governed by ratios can be enlarged from a scroll to a feature wall without distortion, because its proportions travel with it. Our vectorization work depends on exactly this property.
- **Verifiable fidelity:** When a lineage fixes its proportions, fidelity stops being a matter of taste. A translation can be checked against the grid, measure by measure, the way a fabricator checks a shop drawing.
- **Centered composition:** The concentric geometry that iconometry produces gives an interior a natural focal point. A mandala-governed form holds the center of a wall the way a keystone holds an arch.

> The grid is the least visible and most durable thing a lineage owns. Figures fade and pigments age; the ratios are carried out intact.

## The Discipline, Held Honestly

We resist the temptation to claim more than the tradition claims. Iconometry governs sacred figuration in the Himalayan scroll traditions; the folk lineages we steward, Warli above all, work instead by rhythmic freehand repetition, and their vitality depends on it. The two systems are different answers to the same question of how a form survives its maker. One fixes proportion and lets the hand vary. The other fixes the gesture and lets the composition breathe. A responsible curation practice knows which answer it is holding, and never measures one lineage with the other's ruler.
